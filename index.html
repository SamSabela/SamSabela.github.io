Research

Does a better PD model make a better provision?

My PGDip research compares logistic regression with four tree-ensemble families on 618,584 loans, then follows each model's probabilities all the way through to an IFRS 9 impairment charge.

Submitted · November 2026

Probability of default and IFRS 9 expected credit loss: logistic regression against tree ensembles

The study asks three questions the applied credit-risk literature tends to leave unexamined. Do the metrics used to select a PD model answer the question an impairment charge actually poses? What does a defensible look-ahead control cost on a fixed-term product? And when macroeconomic variables are supplied as loan-level features, do they act as conditioning information, or as something else in its place?

Programme
PGDip Data Analytics, IIE Varsity College
Data
Lending Club accepted loans, 36-month term
Design
Three rolling-origin folds, fixed seed, equal budget across families
Controls
Leakage register of 39 excluded fields, plus a univariate AUC screen

Method

DATASample and screen36-month Lending Club loans, filtered through the leakage register
ENRICHMacro featuresSix US and six South African series from FRED, aligned by publication lag
MODELFive familiesLogistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost
EVALUATECompareDiscrimination, calibration, paired DeLong tests with Holm correction
EXPLAINSHAP vs coefficientsTreeSHAP stability checked against logistic regression
PROVISIONIFRS 9 ECL45% LGD on an annuity exposure schedule, discounted at each loan's own rate

The model families

LRThe interpretable baseline banks already defend to regulators.
RFBagging: many deep trees, averaged to cut variance.
GBMBoosting three ways: XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost.

Robustness checks

  • A 36-month look-ahead embargo, so no model trains on an outcome unknown at its cut-off.
  • A temporal placebo to test whether an apparent macro gain is only a date stamp.
  • A rerun without the instalment feature to test for leakage carried in a ratio.

Engineering

  • One config file drives every run; results are deterministic under the seed.
  • A vectorised ECL engine, pinned by tests to a scalar reference implementation.
  • Raw data never committed; macro retrieval logged with provenance.
